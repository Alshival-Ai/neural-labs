# Conversion Architecture

Choose structure from the customer's decision, not from a fixed template. One primary action should dominate; secondary actions should support it rather than compete with it.

## First-viewport contract

The first viewport should answer:

1. Who is this business?
2. What does it offer or solve?
3. Where does it operate, when location matters?
4. Why should the visitor continue?
5. What should the visitor do next?

## Useful section patterns

Select only what the business can support with credible content:

- Hero with primary action and immediate business context
- Proof strip with factual differentiators
- Services, menu, product categories, or signature offerings
- Outcome-focused explanation of the experience or process
- Gallery or portfolio with real work
- About or founder story when it builds trust
- Reviews sourced from an authorized or attributable source
- Team, credentials, licenses, guarantees, or policies
- Location, service area, hours, parking, accessibility, and directions
- FAQ addressing conversion objections
- Final action block and complete footer

Do not insert sections merely to make the page longer. Every section should answer a buying question, establish trust, or move the visitor toward the primary action.

## Category tendencies

- Restaurants and cafés: menu or signature items, atmosphere, hours, location, reservations or ordering, dietary and accessibility information.
- Barbers, salons, spas, and studios: services, pricing when verified, team, portfolio, preparation or policies, booking.
- Tattoo studios: make actual completed tattoos on clients the main visual proof. When suitable source-supported images are available under the project's asset policy, lead with a strong work photo and a varied on-page portfolio; keep the storefront primarily in the visit/location section. Preserve the artwork's color and proportions, choose crops that show the complete piece, and credit source posts or artists when known. A social link complements the gallery; it should not replace all visual work when usable images are available. Never fill a claimed client-work gallery with generated tattoos or unrelated stock. If media is unavailable, document that specific gap instead of presenting a text-only style list as a finished portfolio.
- Contractors and local services: problems solved, service area, process, proof of work, licenses or insurance when verified, quote or call.
- Shops and showrooms: product categories, visit reasons, featured inventory without false availability, hours, directions, contact.
- Clinics and wellness businesses: services, practitioner information, patient expectations, eligibility or insurance only when verified, booking or call; avoid unsupported medical claims.

## Conversion behavior

- Repeat the primary action at natural decision points using consistent language.
- On mobile, consider a compact sticky action only when it does not obscure content and the action is genuinely useful.
- Use `tel:`, `mailto:`, directions, booking, reservation, or quote links only when their destinations are verified.
- Explain what happens after form submission. Do not ship a form without a configured delivery path, validation, success state, error state, and spam strategy.
