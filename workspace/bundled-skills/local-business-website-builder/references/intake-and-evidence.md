# Intake and Evidence

Use this reference when the starting material is a public business listing, screenshots, a lead note, an incomplete client brief, or a mixture of sources.

## Classify the build

- `prospect concept`: A private demonstration intended to show a business what a possible website could look like. Mark unknowns and demo-only behavior clearly. Do not present it as the business's official site or publish it under the business's identity without authorization.
- `new client build`: The business has authorized the work. Treat client-provided information as supplied evidence, but identify contradictions and launch-critical gaps.
- `redesign`: Preserve verified content and working integrations unless replacement is in scope. Record what should be retained, improved, removed, or reverified.
- `audit`: Inspect and report. Do not change the site unless implementation is also requested.

## Normalize the brief

Record the following when relevant:

- Official business name and category
- Primary customer action
- Phone, email, address, service area, and hours
- Services, products, menu, pricing, policies, and differentiators
- Booking, reservation, ordering, directions, quote, or contact destinations
- Logo, brand colors, fonts, photos, video, icons, menus, and documents
- Social profiles and third-party platforms
- Reviews, ratings, licenses, awards, years in business, and other trust claims
- Required pages, languages, integrations, compliance needs, and launch destination

For each item, store a status: `verified`, `client-provided`, `inferred`, `missing`, or `not-applicable`. Preserve source notes or URLs when research was requested.

## Evidence rules

- Treat facts visible on a lead-discovery service as pointers to reverify, not automatically reusable project data. Follow the source's current terms and do not persist or transform content where those terms disallow it.
- Prefer business-provided material, direct confirmation, official business-controlled profiles, government or licensing records where appropriate, and other sources whose terms permit the intended use.
- Do not copy competitor language, branding, photography, menu text, or page design.
- OCR menus, price lists, or service sheets only from user-supplied or permission-cleared material. Do not infer missing items or prices from ambiguous photographs.
- Do not turn a rating into a testimonial or invent a customer quote.
- Do not imply ownership of third-party profiles, awards, affiliations, or certifications.
- If sources disagree, expose the conflict instead of choosing silently.
- Use placeholders such as `[CLIENT TO CONFIRM]` only in briefs or private concepts. Do not ship those strings in a production build.

## Minimum viable brief

Implementation can usually begin when the primary conversion, business category, rough content scope, art direction, and asset status are known. A private prospect concept may continue with clearly labeled representative content. Pause when an unresolved choice would change the page architecture, legal claims, external integration, or production identity.
